# Solar-Panel-Invertors

This repo will explain the security and electrical risks involved with commonly installed Solar Panel Inverters across the UK and wider world.



## Electrical Supply Risk to Equipment

Most people will assume their solar panels and inverters will be fine if installed by a qualified electrician, preferably but not a mandatory requirement, linked to a recognised solar trade body. However this is not always the case.

The main risk from the Electrical Supply Network (ESN), here in the UK known as the National Grid (NG), are power surges. Whilst a massive effort is made to ensure the frequency of the mains remains stable to avoid "collapse" of the NG, localised surges and spikes can temporarily or permanently disable electrical equipment including solar panel inverters. 

Most people will check the amount of electricity that is generated by their solar panels, using the meter that needs to be fitted, typically near the building's central fuse box. After the novelty has worn off checking every day how much has been generated from solar, periodic meter readings are needed to supply the power company with the information they need to calculate how much needs to be paid back from the feed-in tariff. This meter reading could be a quarterly, half yearly or annual meter reading. This is typically the only time the property owner will know a problem has occurred because their meter will not have gone up, or will have only gone up a little bit, depending on when the inverter fault occurred and when in the year it occurred. Even in summer months with quarterly meter readings, it can take two meter readings to notice a problem.

In order to help prove the NG was at fault, sign up to alerts here: https://www.ukpowernetworks.co.uk/power-cut/text-updates
