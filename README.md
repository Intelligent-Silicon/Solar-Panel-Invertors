# Solar-Panel-Invertors

This repo will explain the security and electrical risks involved with commonly installed Solar Panel Inverters across the UK and wider world.



## Electrical Supply Risk to Equipment

Most people will assume their solar panels and inverters will be fine if installed by a qualified electrician, preferably with a recognised accreditation like with a Solar Trade organisation. 

However this isnt always the case.

The main risk from the Electrical Supply Network (ESN), here in the UK known as the National Grid (NG) or Mains, are power surges. 

Whilst a massive effort is made to ensure the frequency (50Hz) of the Mains remains stable to avoid "collapse" of the NG, localised surges and spikes can temporarily or permanently disable electrical equipment including solar panel inverters. 

Most people will check the amount of electricity that is generated by their solar panels, using the meter that UK Govt Regulations demand be fitted, typically near the building's central fuse box. 

After the novelty has worn off checking every day how much has been generated from solar, periodic meter readings are needed by the power company with the amount of electricity generated. This meter reading will typically be a quarterly (4 times a year) meter reading, with an additional annual meter reading in some cases. This is typically the only time the property owner will know a problem has occurred because their meter will not have gone up, or will have only gone up by some of the expected amount for that period. 

Even in summer months with quarterly meter readings, it can take two meter readings for the home owner to notice a problem.

In order to help prove the NG was at fault, sign up to alerts here: https://www.ukpowernetworks.co.uk/power-cut/text-updates
